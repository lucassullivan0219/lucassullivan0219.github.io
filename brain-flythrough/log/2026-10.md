  - Neuron Safari (Minecraft world for neuroscience) and a Minecraft neuron build: Minecraft is already used to teach neuroscience. No ready-made whole-brain voxel world was found.
  - Minecraft Wiki, Controls and Spectator: default keys (WASD, Space up, Shift down, double-tap W to sprint, 1–9 and wheel for the hotbar, E inventory, T chat, / command, F1 hide HUD, F3 debug, F2 screenshot, Esc menu); spectator mode flies through blocks and sees through them. Taken: the whole control scheme (BF-005).
  - FieldTrip `standard_1020.elc` (Oostenveld & Praamstra 2001): 10-20/10-10 positions in MNI mm on the template head, including A1/A2 and M1/M2. Taken: every electrode coordinate and the scalp shape (BF-004).
  - ACNS Guidelines 2 and 3 (2016): 10-10 names (T7/T8 rather than T3/T4); mastoid leads may replace A1/A2 and are labelled M1/M2; A1/A2 leads are often contaminated with artifact; prefer a midline reference when temporal-lobe epilepsy is suspected. HBCL EEG manual (MSU): mastoids placed too low pick up the pulse.
  - Okamoto 2004 and Koessler 2009: scalp positions projected onto the cortex; between-subject spread of about 5–8 mm. Taken: the idea of drawing a line from each electrode to the cortex below it, and the warning that the region under an electrode varies between people. Their tables were not accessible, so the page computes its own projections and does not quote theirs.
  - MNI/ICBM (Mazziotta 2001) and AAL (Tzourio-Mazoyer 2002): the coordinate frame and the approximate positions of the lobes and deep structures.
  - Course slides in `NEURAL_ENGINEERING_LAB/2016week 3 - Chapter 49_0226.ppt` (Campbell Biology ch. 49): text read for the list of regions to include (lobes, limbic system with cingulate gyrus, hypothalamus, anterior thalamus, hippocampus, amygdala; basal ganglia; brainstem; cerebellum; motor, somatosensory and auditory cortex; prefrontal, orbitofrontal, SMA). The file is not published.
  - Gibson 1998 (surface nets), Quilez (distance functions, smooth minimum), Gustavson 2005 (simplex noise): how the model is turned into meshes.

## BF-003 · 2026-10-16 · Decision
- Raised by: Claude
- Decision: build the brain as code. Every region is a signed distance function in MNI millimetres (ellipsoids, tubes, cutting surfaces and noise grooves for gyri). The browser samples them on a grid and builds smooth surfaces (surface nets) or Minecraft-like blocks from the same functions.
- Options considered:
  - (a) Download a real atlas mesh (ICBM152 or fsaverage surfaces, BodyParts3D, Z-Anatomy). More realistic, but each needs a large third-party file downloaded and hosted under its own licence (BodyParts3D is CC BY-SA; FreeSurfer data has its own licence), plus a separate parcellation to colour the regions. Downloading files was not part of the request.
  - (b) Code (chosen). Same coordinate frame as the electrode file, so no fitting is needed. Each region can be edited and named. Smooth and block surfaces come from the same source. Nothing to download apart from three.js.
- Consequence: shapes are simplified. The gyri are not the real named gyri, the hemispheres are mirror images apart from the noise, and region borders are approximate (millimetres to a centimetre). This is stated on the page under Limitations. If a real atlas mesh is wanted later, it can replace the cerebrum surfaces while keeping everything else.
- Key numbers: central sulcus fitted to MNI M1/S1 points (hand knob near |x| 38, y -24, z 58; face area near |x| 58, y -8, z 28; paracentral lobule near y -37); lateral fissure z = -2 - 0.6 y; parieto-occipital border y = -58 to -74; M1 = 11 mm strip in front of the central sulcus, S1 = 11 mm strip behind it.
- Files: `brain-flythrough/js/anatomy.js`, `brain-flythrough/js/mesh.js`
- Status: valid

## BF-005 · 2026-10-16 · Decision
- Raised by: Claude (R3, R4, R9)
- Decision: Minecraft's creative-flight controls, with spectator mode's pass-through flying.
  - W A S D level movement; Space up, Shift down; double-tap W to sprint (2.2×); mouse look with pointer lock.
  - Left click "breaks" (hides) the region under the crosshair; right click "uses" it (info card); middle click "picks" it (show only that region). Z undoes, R restores everything.
  - Hotbar 1–9 and the mouse wheel: nine views (whole brain, lobes, functional areas, deep structures, limbic system, basal ganglia, brainstem and cerebellum, EEG 10-20, left hemisphere only).
  - E inventory (every region: show/hide, shift-click for only this, right-click to fly there, search box like the creative inventory); T or / chat with commands (/tp, /hide, /show, /only, /view, /opacity, /speed, /fov, /render, /color, /where, /link, /electrodes, /list, /help) and Tab completion.
  - F3 debug screen (MNI coordinates, block coordinates, facing, what you are inside and looking at); F1 hide HUD; F2 screenshot; Esc pause menu with Options and Controls screens; B blocks/smooth; V colour by lobes or areas; L name tags.
  - Touch screens: left stick, drag to look, up/down buttons, tap for info, long press to hide (as in Minecraft Pocket Edition).
- ISO practice applied (R9):
  - ISO 9241-110:2020 interaction principles. Conformity with user expectations: Minecraft's default keys. Self-descriptiveness: "Inside" line, crosshair label with distance, compass, F3 screen. Controllability: pause, undo, restore, options. Robustness against use errors: see the next points. Learnability: Controls screen, /help, Tab completion, splash tips.
  - Sprint is not Ctrl, because Ctrl+W closes the browser tab; while the pointer is locked the page also asks before closing. F1 and F3 have letter alternates (H, G) because browsers use those keys.
  - Keys are read with `KeyboardEvent.code`, i.e. by physical position on the keyboard (the ISO/IEC 9995 key positions), so an AZERTY keyboard flies with Z Q S D.
- Status: valid
