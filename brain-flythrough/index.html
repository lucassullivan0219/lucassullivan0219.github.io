Brain Fly-Through

  
Fly through a see-through brain with Minecraft-style controls and see where each region sits under the 10-20 EEG electrodes.

  
Click Fly in, move with WASD, rise with Space, sink with Shift, look with the mouse. 1–9 switch views; E lists every region.


  

    Controls
    
The keys follow Minecraft's defaults for creative flight, and like spectator mode you pass through everything.

    

    
Sprint is a double tap of W, not Ctrl: in a browser, Ctrl+W closes the tab. Keys are read by their position on the keyboard (KeyboardEvent.code), so on an AZERTY keyboard the same physical keys (Z Q S D) move you.

    
On a phone or tablet: the left stick moves, dragging on the right looks around, ▲ ▼ fly up and down, a tap shows what is under the crosshair, and a long press hides it.

    Electrodes and references
    
Eight active electrodes from the 10-20 system, plus the two usual reference choices: ear clips on the earlobes (A1, A2) and electrodes on the mastoid bones behind the ears (M1, M2).

    
Loading…

    
Positions are the standard 10-20 coordinates on the MNI template head (FieldTrip standard_1020.elc, from Oostenveld & Praamstra 2001). In that file A1/A2 sit 5 mm behind and 20 mm below the preauricular points, and M1/M2 25 mm behind and 20 mm below. "Under it" is found in this model by stepping from the electrode toward the middle of the brain until the first brain region; published studies that do this on real MRIs (Okamoto 2004, Koessler 2009) find the projected point varies between people by about 5–8 mm.

    

      Ear clip or mastoid?
      
In this template both sites are about equally far from the brain (earlobe 44 mm to the bottom of the temporal lobe, mastoid 45 mm to the cerebellum), so neither is electrically silent. They differ mainly in how they attach.

      
    
        
  • Ear clip (A1/A2) pinches the earlobe, which hangs on soft tissue. It is quick to attach but moves with the ear. ACNS Guideline 3 notes that A1/A2 leads are often contaminated with artifact, and recommends a midline reference such as Cz instead of the ears when temporal-lobe epilepsy is suspected, because the ears pick up temporal activity.
    • 
        
  • Mastoid (M1/M2) is stuck on the bone behind the ear, so contact is steady. Its nearest brain tissue is the cerebellum. Placed too low, it can pick up the pulse.
    • 
        
  • ACNS Guideline 3 allows mastoid leads to replace A1/A2 in referential montages, labelled M1/M2. Linked ears or linked mastoids average the two sides so neither hemisphere is favoured.
    • 
        
  • No point on the body has zero potential, so every reference adds something to every channel. Write down which one you used.
    • 
      

      
Fly to A1 or M1 (/tp A1) and follow the dashed line: for the references it goes to the nearest brain point, not straight inward.

    

  


  

    Regions in the model
    
The model has cortical lobes, functional areas inside them, deep structures, the brainstem and the cerebellum.

    

  


  

    How the model is built
    
    
      
  • Coordinates. Everything is in MNI millimetres (x right, y front, z up; the origin is near the anterior commissure), the same frame as the electrode file, so electrodes and brain line up without any fitting. F3 shows your position in this frame.
    • 
      
  • Shapes as code. Each region is a signed distance function: a formula that says how far a point is from the surface. The cerebrum is a blend of two ellipsoids per hemisphere (main body and temporal lobe), cut by a floor, a 3 mm longitudinal fissure, a 5 mm lateral fissure down to the insula, and a central sulcus groove. Gyri are narrow grooves along the zero lines of 3D simplex noise.
    • 
      
  • Lobes and areas. Lobes are split along the central sulcus, the lateral fissure and the parieto-occipital line; the central sulcus position is fitted to MNI coordinates of the motor hand knob and face area. Functional areas are bands inside the lobes (for example M1 is the 11 mm strip in front of the central sulcus).
    • 
      
  • Deep structures. Thalamus, basal ganglia, hippocampus, amygdala, ventricles, corpus callosum, brainstem and cerebellum are ellipsoids and tubes placed at atlas positions (AAL / MNI templates).
    • 
      
  • Meshes. The page samples the distance functions on a 0.8–1.4 mm grid and builds smooth surfaces with surface nets, or block surfaces (B) by drawing every exposed voxel face. Surfaces are drawn see-through, brighter at the edges, so shapes stay readable from inside.
    • 
      
  • Scalp. A smooth surface through the 10-10 electrode positions of the same file, with simple ears (for the ear clips) and a nose so you can tell front from back.
    • 
      
  • Code. anatomy.js (shapes, regions, electrodes), mesh.js (meshing), main.js (game). Rendering uses three.js r160.
    • 
    

  


  

    Limitations
    
    
      
  • Shapes are simplified on purpose. Gyri are not the real named gyri, and the two hemispheres are mirror images apart from the noise.
    • 
      
  • Region borders are approximate (a few millimetres to a centimetre). Use it for where things are relative to each other, not for targeting.
    • 
      
  • Skull, meninges, CSF outside the ventricles, blood vessels and white-matter tracts are not drawn.
    • 
      
  • The electrode coordinates are for a template head. On a real person they move with head size and cap placement.
